## Description
Synthesis of prior Even Us Up audits. Recommendation: focus next sprint on the highest-leverage differentiators and adoption blockers, not broad feature parity with Splitwise.

Prioritized list:

Top 3 UX friction points
1. Settlement clarity and actionability (Small-Medium, ~1 day)
   - Problem: users do not immediately understand who owes whom, how to settle, or what to do next.
   - Fix: dashboard-level Settle Up card, clearer Interac-first wording, net balance per person, visible settlement history.
   - Why first: this is core product value, and confusion here kills trust.

2. Onboarding to first expense (Medium, ~1-2 days)
   - Problem: blank-state gap after signup/group creation.
   - Fix: 3-step first-run flow, seeded sample group, guided first expense CTA, smart defaults.
   - Why second: activation is the real bottleneck, more than lack of features.

3. Add expense / split setup complexity, especially on mobile (Medium, ~1 week)
   - Problem: too many decisions and manual split entry too early.
   - Fix: compress split setup into smart defaults + quick split card, reduce scrolling, improve thumb-friendly layout.
   - Why third: directly improves daily use and lowers abandonment.

Top 3 missing features
1. Bill review / audit insights (Medium-Large, ~3-4 weeks)
   - Differentiate with audit-style intelligence: fairness, anomalies, receipt discrepancies, settlement optimization.
   - Strongest moat vs Splitwise because it is not just another ledger.

2. Notifications and reminder loops (Medium, ~2-3 days)
   - Settlement reminders, inactivity nudges, confirmation emails/push.
   - Important for retention once onboarding/settlement UX is fixed.

3. Household / recurring workflows polish (Medium, ~2-4 days if core exists)
   - Lean into couples/roommates/shared-home use cases with recurring templates and a more persistent household mode.
   - Best niche wedge if Joe wants growth, not just maintenance.

Top 3 growth levers
1. Canada-first positioning (Small-Medium, ~1 day)
   - Interac-native, CAD-first, household-friendly messaging.
   - Explicit comparison vs Splitwise on landing page.

2. Household/couples niche focus (Medium, ~1-2 weeks)
   - This is more defensible than generic group-expense competition.
   - Recurring rent/utilities + ongoing shared budgets creates stickiness.

3. Audit/reporting premium layer (Medium-Large, ~3-4 weeks)
   - Monetizable and differentiated. Moves product from utility to insight tool.

Recommendation
- Next sprint should be: (1) settlement clarity, (2) onboarding, (3) mobile/add-expense simplification.
- After that: notifications + household recurring polish.
- Only then invest in bigger moat work like audit/reporting.

Why this order
- Splitwise can copy features, but clarity + onboarding + positioning improve activation now.
- Even Us Up wins by feeling simpler for Canadian households, not by matching every checkbox.

Suggested immediate roadmap
- Week 1: settlement clarity + onboarding
- Week 2: add-expense/mobile simplification
- Week 3: notifications + recurring polish
- Week 4+: audit/reporting prototype
