# Sales Pitch Deck — Atlantic Contractor Portal
**Purpose:** Discovery call reference (talking points, not a formal deck)  
**Audience:** Small contractor teams (5-20 staff)  
**Duration:** 45 minutes (call structure)  
**Tone:** Curious, not salesy. We're learning, not pitching.

---

## OPENING (Minutes 0-5)
**Objective:** Build rapport, set context, get permission to ask questions

### Talking Points

> "Hi [Name], thanks so much for taking time to chat. I really appreciate it.
>
> Quick context: I'm building a lightweight approval portal specifically for small contractor teams in Atlantic Canada. The idea came from talking to teams like yours who handle quotes and scope changes via scattered emails and texts—and it's a manual nightmare.
>
> I'm not here to pitch you anything yet. Honestly, I'm still in the learning phase. I've talked to [X number] of contractors so far, and I'm trying to understand the real pain points so I can build something that actually solves the problem.
>
> If you're open to it, I'd love to ask you some questions about how your team currently handles approvals. Sound good?"

**Their response:** Usually yes. If hesitant, say: "No worries if it's not a great time. When would be better?"

---

## PAIN DISCOVERY (Minutes 5-20)
**Objective:** Understand their current process and frustrations  
**Your job:** Listen 70%, talk 30%  
**Technique:** Ask open questions, then follow up with "Tell me more..."

### Opening Question

> "So when a client wants you to look at a quote or approve a scope change, walk me through how that conversation usually happens."

**Follow-ups (listen to their answer, then ask relevant ones):**

1. **Current tool:** "What are you using to send quotes right now? Email, text, something else?"
2. **Volume:** "On average, how many quotes or scope changes does your team send out per month?"
3. **Back-and-forth:** "When you send a quote, how many messages back-and-forth until the client actually approves? Are we talking 1-2 or more like 5-10?"
4. **Time impact:** "How long does that typically take? A few hours? A day? Multiple days?"
5. **Cost of delay:** "What happens if approval takes longer than expected? Does that impact your timeline?"
6. **Team involvement:** "Who on your team is usually handling this? Just you, or multiple people?"
7. **Pain trigger:** "What's the most frustrating part of the current process?" (Listen for the emotional answer—that's gold.)
8. **Dispute handling:** "Have you ever had a situation where a client said 'I never got that email' or there was confusion about what was approved?"

### Active Listening Cues

- Take notes (shows you're serious)
- Say "Tell me more" when they mention pain
- Nod, make eye contact (even on Zoom, look at camera)
- Repeat back: "So what I'm hearing is... is that right?" (builds trust)
- Don't jump to solutions yet

### Key Data to Capture

- Pain level (1-10): *How much does this frustrate them?*
- Volume: *How many quotes/changes per month?*
- Current tools: *Email? Text? Spreadsheet? Something else?*
- Decision timeline: *How long approval takes?*
- Team size: *How many people involved?*
- Biggest pain point: *Their own words, verbatim—this becomes marketing copy*

---

## SOLUTION EXPLORATION (Minutes 20-35)
**Objective:** Gauge product-market fit without pitching too hard  
**Your job:** Describe the vision, not the feature list

### Introduce the Idea

> "Okay, so based on what you're telling me, what if there was a simple approval portal that worked like this:
>
> You upload a quote or scope change. Your client gets an email with a link. They click the link—no login needed—they review the document, and click one button: 'Approve' or 'Request Change.' That's it.
>
> You get a notification saying '[Client] approved quote for $X,' and everything is documented. No back-and-forth, no lost emails, no disputes about what was approved.
>
> Does that sound like something that would be useful for your team?"

### Follow-ups (based on their reaction)

**If enthusiastic:**
> "What part appeals to you the most?"
> "What would change for you if approval was 50% faster?"

**If hesitant:**
> "What's the concern? Is it the tech side, or something else?"
> "What would make this actually useful?"

**If lukewarm:**
> "What would we need to add or change to make this something you'd actually use?"

### Product-Market Fit Questions

1. **Relevance:** "Does this feel like a real problem you have?"
2. **Utility:** "Would you actually use something like this, or would you stick with email?"
3. **Value:** "If this saved you an hour per week, what would you do with that time?"
4. **Comparison:** "How does this compare to using Procore or another project tool?" (Don't trash competitors; understand their mindset)
5. **Barriers:** "What would make you hesitant to try something like this?"
6. **Integration:** "Would you need this to connect with your accounting software, or would a standalone tool work?"

### Capture

- Product-market fit rating (1-10)
- Most valuable feature
- Barriers to adoption
- Price tolerance (if they volunteer)
- Integration needs

---

## DESIGN-PARTNER RECRUITMENT (Minutes 35-42)
**Objective:** Gauge interest in becoming a paid pilot  
**Your job:** Make it compelling (no risk, high value to them)

### Pitch the Pilot Opportunity

> "Here's what I'm thinking: Based on what you've told me, I think your team would be a perfect fit for something I'm calling a 'design-partner pilot.'
>
> Basically, you'd get early access to this portal, completely free. I'd build it with your workflow in mind—so it does exactly what you need, not generic features. And I'd need you to try it on real projects, give me feedback every week, and tell me what works and what doesn't.
>
> The benefit for you: You get to shape the product, zero implementation cost (that's usually $2-5K), and you're ahead of the curve when we launch.
>
> The benefit for me: Real feedback from real users before we go to market.
>
> Timeline: We'd start in April, go live in May, and run the pilot through June. Then we make a decision about whether to push forward.
>
> Does that sound interesting?"

### If They Say Yes:

> "Awesome. Here's what I'd need from you:
> - Use the portal on at least 3-5 real projects (quotes, scope changes, approvals)
> - Join me for a 30-minute feedback call every Wednesday morning
> - Be honest about what works and what doesn't
> - If you love it, refer 1-2 other contractors after we launch
>
> Does that work for your schedule?"

**Next steps if interested:**
- Confirm timeline (Apr 1 kick-off, May 15 launch)
- Get their top 3 pain points (for design prioritization)
- Confirm team member who'll be primary contact
- Schedule first feedback call (Apr 8 or Apr 15)

### If They Say No:

> "No problem. What would need to be different for you to be interested?"
> 
> (Listen—this is valuable feedback.)
> 
> "Would it be okay if I checked back in a couple months when we have a prototype? I'd love your feedback then."

---

## REFERRALS (Minutes 42-44)
**Objective:** Expand your prospect list  
**Tone:** Grateful, low-pressure

### Ask for Referrals

> "One more thing, and this is super helpful: Do you know any other contractors in Atlantic Canada who have similar challenges with approvals? Someone doing HVAC, plumbing, renovations, snow removal, whatever?"

**If they give referrals:**
> "Thank you. Would you be comfortable with me mentioning that [Prospect Name] referred me?"

**Capture:**
- Referral name, company, service, email
- Why they'd be a good fit
- Permission to use their name

---

## CLOSING (Minutes 44-45)
**Objective:** Leave them with next steps, appreciation

### Thank You + Next Steps

> "Thank you so much for your time and honesty. This has been really helpful.
>
> Here's what I'll do: I'm running these calls through mid-April, and then I'm going to synthesize everything I've learned and make a decision about whether to build this. If I do, I'll be in touch about the pilot.
>
> In the meantime, if anything comes to mind—anything about your approval process, features you wish existed, whatever—just shoot me an email. I read every one.
>
> Take care!"

**Send within 24 hours:** Thank-you email with one quote they said that resonated + a brief summary of what you learned

## COMMON OBJECTIONS + RESPONSES

### "We're already using Procore / Asana / Monday.com"

**Your response:**
> "Got it. What's one thing you wish [Tool] did better for your approval workflow specifically? Like, if you could add one feature, what would it be?"
>
> [Listen—this informs your positioning, not a deal-killer. Many contractors use multiple tools.]

**Why it works:** Shows you're not competitive, you're collaborative. Also reveals pain even Procore can't solve (billing integration, bilingual templates, etc.).

### "This sounds like something someone else is probably doing"

**Your response:**
> "You're right, there are definitely platforms out there. But what I'm noticing from talking to teams like yours is that most tools are designed for big construction firms with complex workflows. You guys need something simpler—just approvals, no bloat.
>
> Plus, I'm building this specifically for Atlantic Canada, with bilingual templates and workflows that make sense for micro-teams. That's the gap I'm going after.
>
> But you're right to be skeptical. What would I need to show you to convince you this is worth trying?"

**Why it works:** Acknowledges the concern, positions you as specialist (not competitor), invites their input.

### "I'm too busy right now"

**Your response:**
> "I totally get it. I'm not asking for a huge commitment. Just 45 minutes, and that's it. But if now's not a good time, when would be better? Next week? Next month?"

**Why it works:** Removes time pressure, shows flexibility.

### "We need to see it before committing"

**Your response:**
> "That's totally fair. Here's the thing: I'm still in the discovery phase right now, so there's nothing to see yet. But once I finish these 10 calls, I'll have a rough prototype in 4 weeks. Would you be interested in seeing it then? No obligation—I just want to get your feedback."

**Why it works:** Sets realistic expectations, keeps them engaged, respects their decision-making process.

### "How much does it cost?"

**Your response:**
> "Still figuring that out. The working idea is $75-150 per month depending on features and team size. But honestly, I want to nail the value prop first—make sure this solves your actual problem—before we talk pricing. Your feedback helps me figure out what's worth paying for.
>
> Does that price range feel reasonable if it saved you 5+ hours per week?"

**Why it works:** Gives ballpark (not vague), frames it around value, asks a closing question to gauge interest.

## THINGS TO REMEMBER

### What You're Doing Right
- ✅ You're curious, not salesy
- ✅ You're listening more than talking
- ✅ You're solving a real pain point
- ✅ You're offering something free (no risk)
- ✅ You're building with THEM, not for THEM

### What Kills Discovery Calls
- ❌ Pitching features before understanding pain
- ❌ Talking more than listening
- ❌ Being vague about timeline or commitment
- ❌ Dismissing their current tools
- ❌ Making them feel like a sales prospect (you're not selling, you're learning)

### How to Know If It's a Good Call
- They answered all your questions candidly
- They told you their biggest pain point (emotional, specific)
- They asked questions about the product (sign of interest)
- You have 3+ referral names
- You know whether they're a pilot candidate or not
