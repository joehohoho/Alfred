# Discovery Call Example — Reference
**Purpose:** Show what a successful call looks like and how to fill CALL-TRACKING.md  
**Scenario:** Simulated call with a fictional contractor (based on research patterns)  
**Duration:** ~45 minutes

---

## SIMULATED CALL #1 — Michael Clarke, Clarke Heating & Cooling

**Setup:**
- **Date:** Tuesday, April 1, 2026
- **Time:** 2:30 PM AST (Zoom)
- **Prospect:** Michael Clarke, owner of Clarke Heating & Cooling (Halifax, NS)
- **Company size:** 7 employees
- **Service:** HVAC installation and maintenance

---

## CALL TRANSCRIPT (EXCERPT)

### Opening (0-5 min)

**Alfred:**
> "Hi Michael, thanks so much for taking time to chat today. I really appreciate it. Quick context: I'm building a lightweight approval portal for small contractor teams in Atlantic Canada to replace scattered emails and texts when clients need to approve quotes. I'm not here to pitch—I'm learning. Would you have 45 minutes to chat about your current process?"

**Michael:**
> "Yeah, sure. Always looking for ways to streamline things. Go ahead."

---

### Pain Discovery (5-20 min)

**Alfred:**
> "Great. So when a client wants to approve a quote or scope change, walk me through how that conversation usually happens."

**Michael:**
> "Oh man, it's a nightmare. So we'll do a site visit, measure up the job, write the estimate in Word or Excel, then email it to the customer. Then they either reply by email or call back with questions. Sometimes they want changes—'Can you do it cheaper?' or 'Can you add this?'—so we send back another revised quote. Sometimes it takes 5, 6 back-and-forths before we get approval. And half the time the customer is like, 'Wait, I thought you said $8,000, not $8,500' and it turns into a big thing."

**Alfred:** *(taking notes)*
> "So email is the main thing. How many quotes are you sending out per month, roughly?"

**Michael:**
> "Honestly, depends on the season. Summer we might do 15-20 quotes a month. Winter, maybe 5-10. We're not some huge outfit—it's me, two techs, and an office manager."

**Alfred:**
> "And when you send a quote, how long does it usually take to get approval? Days? Weeks?"

**Michael:**
> "I'd say average is 3-5 business days. But some jobs sit for a week or two. The longer it sits, the more likely the customer forgets about it or finds someone else."

**Alfred:** *(key insight)*
> "So speed matters to you?"

**Michael:**
> "Yeah, absolutely. We estimate that losing a job to slow approval costs us maybe $2,000-3,000 on average. And it happens maybe 2-3 times a year. So that's real money."

**Alfred:**
> "Definitely real. And you mentioned your office manager—does she handle most of the back-and-forth, or is it you?"

**Michael:**
> "She handles a lot of it, but then customers call me with questions, and we end up in these weird email chains where nobody knows what the latest version is. We tried using Dropbox for a while to keep files organized, but customers don't check Dropbox. They just want email."

**Alfred:** *(understanding their workflow)*
> "Got it. So if I had to sum it up—the pain is: quotes take forever to get approved, there's confusion about versions, and you lose jobs because of delays. Does that sound right?"

**Michael:**
> "Yeah, that's exactly it. We lose maybe 10% of quotes just because the back-and-forth takes too long, and the customer gets tired of dealing with us."

---

### Solution Exploration (20-35 min)

**Alfred:**
> "Okay, so what if there was a simple portal where you could upload a quote, your customer gets an email with a link, they click it—no login—and they see the quote and just click 'Approve' or 'Request Change'? And you get a notification saying 'Michael Clarke approved quote for $8,200 on March 31st.' That's it. Everything is recorded."

**Michael:**
> "Hmm. That's simpler than I'd have thought. But would customers actually use it? Like, would they click a link and approve something?"

**Alfred:**
> "That's what I'm trying to figure out. In theory, it's one click vs. writing an email reply. What do you think—would your customers do it?"

**Michael:**
> "I think... yeah, actually. If it was simple enough. The thing I worry about is: What if they don't get the email? Or what if there's some error and I think they approved it but they didn't?"

**Alfred:**
> "Good question. So you'd need a clear audit trail—'Michael Clarke approved this quote on this date at this time.' You'd see it in the portal so there's no confusion later."

**Michael:**
> "Okay, yeah. That would be helpful. We've actually had disputes where the customer said they never got the email, or they thought it was just a quote and not a binding approval. If we had a record, that would solve that."

**Alfred:** *(capturing product-market fit)*
> "So clarity and speed. Those are the big wins?"

**Michael:**
> "Yeah. And honestly, if approval came in 24 hours instead of 5 days, that would change our business. We could schedule crews better, quote more jobs because we're not waiting around."

**Alfred:**
> "That's helpful. One more thing—would you need this integrated with anything? Like, do you use accounting software or project management?"

**Michael:**
> "We use... honestly, mostly spreadsheets. We do use QuickBooks for invoicing, but I don't think I need it integrated with that. Just something simple to manage approvals would be huge."

---

### Design-Partner Recruitment (35-42 min)

**Alfred:**
> "Okay, last thing. Based on what you've told me, I think Clarke Heating & Cooling would be a perfect fit for something I'm calling a 'design-partner pilot.' Basically, I'd build this with your workflow in mind, you'd try it on real jobs for a couple months, give me feedback every week, and you'd get it completely free. Normally, implementation for a tool like this costs $2-5K."

**Michael:**
> "Wait, free? Why would you do that?"

**Alfred:**
> "Because I need real users to test it and tell me what works and what doesn't. You get early access shaped around your needs. I get the feedback I need to build the right thing."

**Michael:**
> "That actually sounds... not bad. What would I have to do?"

**Alfred:**
> "Use it on your next 3-5 jobs. Send me feedback every Wednesday—30 minutes. Tell me what works, what's broken, what would make it better. That's it."

**Michael:**
> "Yeah, I could do that. We've got jobs lined up, so testing it shouldn't be hard. When would this start?"

**Alfred:**
> "Ideally April 1st. I'd have something ready by mid-May. You'd use it through end of June. Then we decide if it's worth paying for."

**Michael:**
> "Sounds good. I'm in."

---

### Referrals (42-44 min)

**Alfred:**
> "That's awesome. One quick thing—do you know any other contractors in Atlantic Canada with similar pain points? Someone doing plumbing, renovations, whatever?"

**Michael:**
> "Actually, yeah. My buddy Tom runs a snow removal and landscaping business in Dieppe. He's always complaining about approvals. And there's a renovation crew in Saint John I know from a chamber event. Both would probably be interested."

**Alfred:**
> "Would I be able to mention you referred me?"

**Michael:**
> "Yeah, absolutely."

---

### Closing (44-45 min)

**Alfred:**
> "Michael, this has been super helpful. Thank you. I'm going to finish these calls by mid-April, make a decision about whether to build this, and if I do, I'll be in touch about the pilot. Deal?"

**Michael:**
> "Sounds good. I'm excited about this."

**Alfred:**
> "Me too. Take care!"

## DATA CAPTURED FROM CALL

### Pain Discovery
- **Current process:** Email quotes, multiple back-and-forths, confusion about versions
- **Pain level (1-10):** 8/10 (losing jobs to slow approvals, customer disputes)
- **Monthly volume:** 15-20 summer, 5-10 winter (avg ~12 per month)
- **Key pain points:**
  - Approval takes 3-5 days (loses 10% of quotes due to slowness)
  - Version confusion (customers unsure which is "final")
  - Disputes about approvals ("I never got that email")
  - Lost revenue: ~$6,000-9,000 per year
- **Current tool:** Email + Word/Excel + spreadsheets for tracking
- **Estimated revenue impact:** 10% quote loss = $30-40K annual impact

### Solution Exploration
- **Product-market fit (1-10):** 8/10 (clear need for speed + clarity)
- **Most valuable feature:** Approval audit trail (clear "approved on X date" record)
- **Integration needs:** None immediately (QuickBooks nice-to-have, not critical)
- **Willingness to try:** YES—enthusiastic
- **Price sensitivity:** Not discussed (said he'd need to see it, but no objection to paid model)
- **Barriers to adoption:** None identified; he sees clear value

### Design-Partner Interest
- **Interested in pilot?** YES
- **Why:** Free implementation (normally $2-5K), shaped to his workflow, solves real pain
- **Timeline feasibility:** YES—"we've got jobs lined up"
- **Team involvement needed:** Just Michael (owner/manager) + office manager for feedback
- **Signed up for:** Weekly feedback calls (Wednesdays)
- **Start date:** Apr 1, 2026
- **Duration:** Apr 1 - Jun 30, 2026

### Referrals
- **Referral #1:** Tom (snow removal/landscaping, Dieppe, NB) — referred by Michael
- **Referral #2:** Renovation crew (Saint John, NB) — referred by Michael
- Permission given to use Michael's name

### Validation Score
- **Pain >= 7?** ✅ YES (8/10)
- **Product-fit >= 7?** ✅ YES (8/10)
- **Design-partner possibility?** ✅ YES — SIGNED

**TOTAL VALIDATION SCORE: 8/10 — STRONG GO**

### Key Quote (for marketing)
